Houston Pest Control Seasonal Calendar

Pest control in the Houston metro works better when it is timed than when it is reactive, and the timing is reasonably predictable. The region's humid subtropical climate gives most pests a long active season and a short, unreliable dormancy, so the useful question is not whether a pest is present but when treating it does the most good. This calendar sets out what we actually see month by month across Jersey Village, Houston, Cypress, Spring, Katy and Tomball, and what each window is the right moment to treat.

Direct Answer: The Two Windows That Matter Most

Early spring (February to March) is when treatment prevents the year's populations from establishing: termite pre-treatment, the first fire ant broadcast bait and wasp eave treatment all land before colonies grow. Late autumn (October to November) is when rodent exclusion has to be finished, before cooling nights drive rats and mice indoors. A plan that hits those two windows properly handles most of the year.

Spring: February to May

Spring is the busiest treatment season here, and almost everything starts at once. Subterranean termites swarm after warm rain from March into May, which is when most Houston homeowners discover an existing colony: a scatter of discarded wings on a windowsill is the classic first sign. Fire ants resume foraging as soil warms, making February and March the most effective broadcast baiting window of the year. Mosquito breeding restarts with the first warm standing water, and paper wasp and yellowjacket queens select nest sites on eaves and in soffits, which is why eave treatment in early spring prevents so much work in August. Argentine ants and odorous house ants move indoors during spring rain, following moisture into kitchens and bathrooms.

What to do: schedule termite inspection before swarm season rather than after it, get the first fire ant broadcast bait down in February or March, and have exterior eaves treated before nests are founded.

Summer: June to August

Summer is peak pressure and peak reinfestation speed. Mosquito populations spike three to five days after every significant rain, and the long days plus standing water in detention basins and retention ponds keep them continuous rather than episodic. Fire ant mounds are active but less visible as colonies move deeper in the heat, which makes midsummer a poor baiting window compared with spring and autumn. Roaches, both American cockroaches entering from sewers and drains and German cockroaches already resident indoors, reach their fastest reproductive rates. Fleas peak in the warm, humid conditions, particularly in homes with pets using the yard daily. Scorpions and spiders move indoors in the driest stretches of August looking for moisture.

What to do: this is the window where in-season monthly mosquito service earns its place over quarterly treatment alone, and where flea and tick work needs its follow-up visit scheduled properly rather than skipped.

Autumn: September to November

Autumn is the most misread season in the Houston area, because mosquito activity drops and homeowners assume pest pressure has ended, while in reality two of the year's sharpest pressures arrive. Stinging insect colonies reach their maximum size and defensiveness from late August into October, and foraging wasps turn to scavenging around bins and patios as natural food declines, which is why most sting incidents happen now rather than in spring. Then from late October, falling night temperatures push roof rats and field mice toward structures, and attics, garages and soffit returns are where they arrive first. Fire ants get a second good baiting window in September and October as foraging resumes near the surface.

What to do: have rodent exclusion inspected and completed in October, not December, because exclusion is far easier before animals are nesting inside. Deal with stinging insect nests while they are still accessible, and get the second fire ant bait down.

Winter: December to January

Winter is quieter outdoors and busier indoors. Rodent pressure stays at its annual peak through February. German cockroaches continue breeding at full rate inside heated structures, entirely indifferent to the outside temperature. Overwintering wasp queens and occasional invaders such as beetles and silverfish appear during warm spells, and subterranean termites remain active underground even though they are not swarming. Mosquito and fire ant activity genuinely do fall away, and in most winters there is no sustained freeze deep enough to reduce populations for the following spring.

What to do: use the quiet exterior season for interior and structural work, which is exactly what this window is good for. Rodent exclusion, interior roach treatment and reviewing your recurring plan for the year ahead all fit better now than in June.

How This Maps Onto a Service Plan

A quarterly plan works in the Houston metro when the four visits are timed against this calendar rather than dropped at even ninety-day intervals: a late-winter or early-spring visit ahead of termite swarm and fire ant foraging, an early-summer visit as mosquito and roach pressure climbs, an autumn visit weighted toward exclusion and stinging insects, and a winter visit focused on interior and structural work. That is how we schedule plan customers, and it is the practical difference between a recurring plan and four identical sprays. Properties with standing water, greenbelt frontage or a history of heavy pressure usually add monthly mosquito service between April and October on top of it. Common Questions

What is the single most important month for pest control in Houston?

March, because it is the month where the most pressures start at once: termite swarms begin, fire ant foraging resumes, mosquito breeding restarts and wasp queens found their nests. Treatment applied in March is acting before populations establish, which is cheaper and more effective than reacting in July. If you only act once a year, act in early spring.

Does pest pressure in Houston actually stop in winter?

It changes rather than stops. Mosquito and ant activity falls off, but rodent pressure peaks from late October through February as animals move indoors, German cockroaches continue breeding year-round in heated structures, and overwintering queens and occasional invaders keep showing up in warm spells. Winter is the best window for rodent exclusion and interior work precisely because the outdoor pests are quiet.

Should I switch to monthly service in summer?

It depends on the property rather than the season alone. Homes backing onto standing water, greenbelt or a drainage easement, and homes with a history of heavy mosquito or fire ant pressure, usually get a clear benefit from monthly service between April and October. A typical subdivision lot without those features is generally well covered by a quarterly plan with the visits timed to the seasonal peaks.
