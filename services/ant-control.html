Ant and Fire Ant Control in Jersey Village, TX

Ants are the most common call we take across the Houston metro, and they are also the pest most often made worse by DIY treatment. Jersey Village Pest Control Pros identifies the species first, because fire ants, Argentine ants and odorous house ants each respond to a different approach, and the wrong product can scatter a colony instead of removing it.

Direct Answer: Mound Treatment or Yard Treatment?

For fire ants, treat the whole yard, not the mounds. Broadcast bait applied twice a year (spring and late summer) gives season-long control, while treating mounds individually simply moves the problem a few feet as neighbouring colonies expand into the space.

The Three Ants That Matter in Houston

Red imported fire ants dominate open sunny turf across Harris County. They build loose soil mounds that appear overnight after rain and sting in coordinated groups when the mound is disturbed. They are the main safety concern for families with small children and pets.

Argentine ants form very large connected colonies with multiple queens, trailing along foundations, fence lines and irrigation lines. They are the species most likely to appear indoors in long steady trails, and the species most likely to bud into satellite colonies if sprayed with a repellent product.

Odorous house ants are small, dark and give off a distinctive smell when crushed. They move indoors in numbers during heavy rain and during dry spells, following moisture into kitchens and bathrooms.

What Our Ant Treatment Includes

  • Species identification during the initial inspection, including trail-following to locate the nest or the entry point
  • Yard-wide broadcast fire ant bait rather than mound-by-mound treatment, timed for the spring and late-summer foraging peaks
  • Non-repellent perimeter application around the foundation, which foragers carry back through the colony instead of avoiding
  • Targeted indoor baiting at active trails, with no broadcast spraying of living space
  • Treatment of common nesting harbourage: mulch beds, landscape timbers, irrigation boxes and the base of exterior walls
  • Guidance on trimming vegetation contact with the structure, which is the single most effective thing a homeowner can change

Why Houston Yards Reinfest So Quickly

Harris County has one of the highest fire ant densities in the United States, often exceeding 200 mounds per acre in untreated turf. When you kill one mound, the territory it occupied is quickly divided between surrounding colonies, and within a few weeks the visible mound count is back where it started. Yard-wide baiting works because foragers carry the bait back to the queen across every colony on the property at once.

Rainfall drives the other half of the pattern. Heavy Gulf storms flood shallow nests and push both fire ants and Argentine ants toward higher, drier ground, which frequently means a home's foundation and slab edge. The surge of indoor ant activity two or three days after a major rain event is the most predictable seasonal call we get in Jersey Village, Cypress and Katy.

Common Questions

How do I get rid of fire ant mounds for good?

Individual mound treatment kills that colony but neighbouring colonies move in within weeks, which is why broadcast bait applied across the whole yard twice a year gives far better long-term control than treating mounds as they appear. Most Houston-area yards need a spring and late-summer application to stay clear.

What is the difference between sugar ants and fire ants in Houston?

The small ants trailing across a kitchen counter are usually odorous house ants or Argentine ants, which nest outdoors and forage indoors for moisture and sweets. Fire ants build visible soil mounds in open sun and sting aggressively when disturbed. They need different products: baits matched to the feeding preference of the species rather than a single spray.

Will spraying ants indoors make the problem worse?

It can. Spraying a visible trail of Argentine or odorous house ants kills foragers but can trigger the colony to bud into several satellite colonies, which spreads the problem. Non-repellent products and baits that foragers carry back to the nest are the reliable approach for these species.
